About

I am a third-year undergraduate in the Department of Organic Materials Engineering at Chungnam National University. I am interested in how polymers can be used to design the interface where the electrode meets the electrolyte.

In the summer of 2026 I built LCO cathode half cells myself at the Korea Battery Academy and ran them through electrochemical testing. Working through that, I saw first-hand that the force with which the binder holds the active material to the current collector shapes cell performance. I am now learning how to look at that interface more precisely.

Electrode binders and interfacial adhesion
How binder composition affects adhesion strength and cycle life
Gel and quasi-solid polymer electrolytes
Ion transport behaviour and interfacial stability

Korea Battery Academy · Jul–Aug 2026

LiCoO2 : Super P : PVDF = 94:3:3 slurry dispersion → viscosity checked at 384 s-1 → doctor-blade coating → vacuum drying at 120°C → roll pressing → 14Ø punching → CR2032 assembly in an Ar glovebox → Formation · C-rate 0.1–2.0C · cycle life

Three cells punched from the same sheet gave initial capacities of 2.65 / 3.74 / 3.85 mAh — a spread of up to 45% — and the composite layer partly delaminated from the Al current collector after pressing. I would like to look further into how binder content relates to electrode adhesion.
